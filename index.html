📱 FOMO and FOBO: The Hidden Fears of the Digital Age

Before the internet, people didn't know what their classmates were doing every evening. Today, we see every party, every trip and every concert in our feed — and this creates a new kind of stress. Psychologists call it FOMO, the fear of missing out.

"I see my friends' stories every day," says 15-year-old Alina. "If they go to a café without me, I feel awful. My life seems not as exciting as theirs, even though I know it's not true."

But FOMO has a younger brother: FOBO — the fear of better options. It appears when we have too many choices and can't make a decision. We spend an hour choosing a film on a streaming service and end up watching nothing. We order food, then feel disappointed because another restaurant probably had better dishes.

"I can't choose anything," admits 16-year-old Mark. "I scroll for hours because I'm afraid the next video will be funnier or more interesting. In the end, I'm more exhausted than relaxed."

Experts say both fears make teenage life more stressful than it was in the past. Their advice is simple: put your phone away, do a digital detox at the weekend, and remember that real life is not as perfect as it looks on a screen. The happiest people are those who don't compare themselves with others — they just enjoy the moment.
